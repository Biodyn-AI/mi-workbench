# Verification: A11 (fine-tuned scGPT vs linear baseline, Replogle K562 CRISPRi, unseen perturbations)

Files: `artifacts/A11-flawed.md`, `artifacts/A11-clean.md`, `ground_truth/A11-flawed.json`, `ground_truth/A11-clean.json`.

Verdict: FIXED (minor). There was one wording edit to each artifact and one clarification to a ground-truth description.

## Checks
1. **Quotes.** All 4 quotes match the artifact verbatim, and each occurs exactly once (re-checked after the edits). F1 is in Interpretation, with the metric defined in Methods. F2 is in Methods, F3 in Data and model, and F4 in Results. None is in Limitations. The clean file has `"flaws": []`.
2. **Planted flaws.**
   - F1 S3: the sole metric is Pearson correlation on absolute mean log-expression across 5,741 genes, and r = 0.989 is called "near-perfect accuracy". There is no delta-based metric and no no-change baseline. Valid metric misuse, detectable from the Methods ("observed profile was the mean log-normalized expression"). The criterion requires the saturation by baseline expression to be identified.
   - F2 C3: scGPT's learning rate and epoch are selected on the 212 test perturbations, while the linear baseline is tuned by CV within training. Valid and explicit. The criterion is strict.
   - F3 T2: scGPT whole-human is given as 24 layers, 16 heads and 1,024 d, with "first 18 of 24 layers frozen". The real checkpoint has 12 layers, 8 heads and 512 d. The error matters because it makes the reported fine-tuning scheme impossible. Valid.
   - F4 B2: HSPA5/BiP knockdown is reported to decrease DDIT3, HERPUD1, DNAJB9 and SEL1L, and BiP is said to "activate" ERN1, EIF2AK3 and ATF6. In fact BiP holds all three sensors inactive, so its knockdown induces the UPR. Both the direction and the mechanism are inverted. Valid.
   - None of the flaws is hedged or signposted. The Limitations entries (one cell line or modality, single perturbations only, no randomly initialized control, knockdown filter) are genuine and separate from the flaws.
3. **Unplanned flaws.** None found.
   - HVGs and linear PCs are computed from control and training data only.
   - The split is by perturbation.
   - There is a single test with no subgroup testing, so no S1.
   - The pretraining explanation is hedged, and its non-isolation is acknowledged.
   - The other biology is correct (ribosomal and Pol II knockdowns give the largest shifts).
4. **Model facts.** Apart from F3 everything is correct: 33M CELLxGENE cells, and the perturbation-flag fine-tuning protocol with MSE loss and paired control inputs.
5. **Clean counterpart.**
   - F1 fixed: Pearson Δ on the top 20 genes plus all genes, MSE, and direction accuracy, with no-change and training-mean baselines. Absolute r is reported to show it does not separate methods (no-change 0.986).
   - F2 fixed: a 106-perturbation validation split for both models, with the test set evaluated once.
   - F3 fixed: 12/8/512, with 8 of 12 layers frozen.
   - F4 fixed: UPR induction with the correct BiP mechanism.
   - Statistics: Holm correction over two prespecified comparisons, and effect sizes with CIs interpreted in proportion ("compatible with no difference or a small linear advantage").
   - No flaw types found.
   - Length: 1,090 vs 1,000 words (ratio 1.09). The tone and structure match the flawed version.
6. **Internal consistency.**
   - Flawed table: weighted means reproduce 0.989 and 0.968, and 55 + 136 = 191 = 90.1%. The Wilcoxon p = 2.1e-31 is attainable with n = 212 (maximum z = 12.6).
   - Clean table: subgroup-weighted means reproduce 0.54, 0.57 and 0.49. The CIs contain the point estimates, the differences match (−0.03 and +0.05), and the Holm-adjusted p-values are compatible with the CIs.
7. **Meta-commentary.** None.

## Fixes made
- A11-flawed and A11-clean, Results: "whose knockdown gave one of the strongest responses in the screen (412 DE genes)" now reads "whose knockdown gave a strong response (412 DE genes)". The same paragraph says ribosomal and Pol II knockdowns produce the largest shifts, and 412 DE genes out of 5,741 is strong but not top-of-screen.
- ground_truth/A11-flawed.json, F1 description: "a no-change prediction ... would also score ~0.99" now reads "would typically also score ~0.98-0.99, comparable to both models". The description no longer implies an exact number that the flawed text does not state.

## Residual concerns
- In the flawed version the linear baseline's absolute r (0.968) is probably below what a no-change predictor would reach (about 0.986 in the clean scenario). This is not stated in the flawed text. A reviewer who notices it would still satisfy F1 ("no-change baseline would score similarly or higher"). It was left unchanged, because narrowing the scGPT–linear gap would invite an S2-type reading that overlaps with F1.
- The scGPT–linear gap (0.021 on a saturated metric, p = 2e-31) may also draw S2-style comments. The F1 criterion is written around saturation of absolute expression, so a pure effect-size critique would not match F1 by itself.
